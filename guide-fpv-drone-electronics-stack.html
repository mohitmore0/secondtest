The FPV drone electronics stack explained

By Space and Tech Engineering LLP | Updated 2026-11-26

Quick answer: An FPV electronics stack has five core parts: flight controller, ESC, receiver, video transmitter and power distribution. Pick them together so voltage, protocol and size all match.

In this guide
  1. The core parts
  2. Plan your build in this order
  3. Common mistakes to avoid
  4. Indian-made options
  5. FAQ

The core parts

  • Flight controller: reads sensors and runs the flight firmware.
  • ESC: drives each brushless motor from the flight controller commands.
  • Receiver: brings your radio link into the drone, for example an ELRS receiver.
  • Video transmitter (VTX): sends the camera feed to your goggles.
  • Power: battery, XT60 connector, regulators and a clean 5V and 9-12V supply.

Plan your build in this order

Start with the frame and motor size. Choose the ESC for the motor current and battery voltage. Then pick the flight controller with enough UARTs for the receiver, VTX and GPS. Finally choose the receiver and VTX that fit your radio and goggles.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Mixing stack sizes that do not fit the frame.
  • Running out of UARTs after buying the board.
  • Putting the GPS or compass next to power wires.
  • Skipping the props-off test before the first flight.

Frequently asked questions

What is a flight stack?

It is the set of boards stacked in the middle of a quad: usually a flight controller and an ESC, sometimes with a VTX or power board.

Do I need an all-in-one board?

All-in-one boards save weight and wiring on compact builds. A separate FC and ESC stack is easier to repair and upgrade.

Which receiver protocol should I use?

ExpressLRS is popular for low latency and long range. Choose the protocol that matches your radio transmitter.
